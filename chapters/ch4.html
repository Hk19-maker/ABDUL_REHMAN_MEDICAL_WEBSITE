Chapter 4

The 17th and 18th centuries marked a turning point. Scientific observation replaced superstition. Anatomy studies became more accurate through systematic dissection. William Harvey’s discovery of blood circulation transformed medical understanding. Hospitals became centers of learning and clinical observation. Surgeons began documenting procedures and outcomes. Despite these improvements, surgery was still extremely painful and dangerous due to the absence of anesthesia and infection control. Operations were often performed quickly to minimize suffering. However, the scientific mindset of this era laid the groundwork for future breakthroughs.
