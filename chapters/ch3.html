chapter 3

During the Middle Ages, scientific progress slowed in many parts of the world. Surgery was often considered less prestigious than academic medicine. Many trained physicians avoided performing operations because they were risky and painful. As a result, barber-surgeons became responsible for many procedures. These individuals performed bloodletting, tooth extraction, wound treatment, and minor surgeries. Though lacking formal medical education, they preserved practical surgical skills. On battlefields, surgical care became crucial. Wars created a demand for trauma treatment, amputation, and wound management. Although outcomes were often poor, battlefield experiences contributed to practical surgical knowledge. This period did not produce major scientific breakthroughs, but it preserved techniques that later evolved into modern surgical methods.
