Chapter 1

Surgery is one of the most demanding, disciplined, and respected branches of medicine. It focuses on diagnosing and treating diseases, injuries, and deformities through operative procedures. Unlike many other medical specialties that rely primarily on medication, surgery involves direct physical intervention in the human body. It requires knowledge, precision, responsibility, and calm decision-making under pressure. From my perspective as a student, surgery represents a balance between science and skill. A surgeon must understand anatomy, physiology, and pathology in depth, but knowledge alone is not enough. Surgery also demands steady hands, critical thinking, teamwork, and emotional strength. A surgeon must know not only what to do, but when to do it — and equally important, when not to operate. Surgery did not begin in modern operating rooms. It developed slowly over centuries through observation, trial, and experience. Early practitioners worked without anesthesia, without antiseptics, and without advanced instruments. Yet their efforts built the foundation of modern surgical science. Understanding this history allows students to appreciate the discipline and progress behind today’s safe and controlled procedures. This book is written to guide students who are beginning their journey into surgical knowledge. It presents the historical development of surgery, explains how surgeons are trained, introduces basic first aid principles, and explores the future of surgical practice. The aim is to provide clarity, structure, and motivation for learners who aspire to understand or enter this challenging field. Surgery is not only a profession — it is a responsibility toward human life. Through this guide, students can develop a foundational understanding of what surgery represents and what it demands from those who choose this path.
